Weston-super-Mare is around twenty miles north west of our base in Wells and it is one of the more demanding places we work, because coastal roofs do not age the way inland ones do.

We cover GRP fibreglass, EPDM rubber and felt across BS22, BS23 and BS24, on houses, flats, guest houses and commercial premises.

What Weston rooftops are usually like

Weston has a large stock of Victorian and Edwardian seafront and near-seafront property, much of it converted into flats or guest accommodation, with flat roofs over rear additions, bay windows, porches and later extensions. It also has a very large number of bungalows and post-war houses further back from the front.

What makes the town different is exposure. Onshore wind, wind-driven rain and salt-laden air work on edge trims, flashings and any lifted seam far harder than they would inland. Roofs here tend to fail at the perimeter and the detailing first, rather than in the middle.

What we get called out for in Weston-super-Mare

  • Edge trims and flashings lifted or loosened by onshore wind
  • Wind-driven rain getting in at upstands and around rooflights
  • Felt roofs on seafront and near-seafront property breaking down faster than expected
  • Flat roofs over rear additions on converted Victorian and Edwardian houses
  • Bungalow extension and garage roofs across the newer estates
  • Repeated patch repairs that have stopped holding and need a proper re-cover

Flat roofing questions we get asked in Weston-super-Mare

Does being near the sea change which covering I should use?

It changes the detailing more than the covering. Fixings, edge trims and upstands need to be right, because that is where coastal roofs fail. Both GRP and EPDM perform well here when they are detailed and fixed properly for the exposure.

Do you work on flats and guest houses?

Yes. A lot of the property near the front is converted or in commercial use, and we work on both. We can also deal with a managing agent or freeholder if that is who is instructing the work.

My roof only leaks when the wind is in a certain direction. Is that normal?

It is a very common pattern on the coast, and it usually points at the detailing rather than the middle of the roof. It is worth mentioning when you call, because it helps us know where to look first.
