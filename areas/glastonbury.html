What Glastonbury rooftops are usually like

The older parts of Glastonbury are built in local lias stone, with a High Street of listed and period buildings where the flat roof work tends to be small, hidden and fiddly. Rear outriggers, shop and cafe extensions, lightwells, and lead-detailed sections behind parapets are all common.

Move out from the centre and the housing changes to twentieth century estates and bungalows, where flat roofs are mostly garages, kitchen extensions and dormers. A lot of these still carry their original felt, which is well past the age where it can be relied on.

What we get called out for in Glastonbury

  • Split or blistered felt on extension and garage roofs laid in the 1970s and 1980s
  • Standing water that never clears, usually a falls problem rather than a covering problem
  • Leaks around rooflights and soil pipes where the old detailing has perished
  • Flat roofs over shop and cafe extensions in and around the High Street
  • Damp showing on an internal ceiling that turns out to be a roof edge or flashing issue rather than the covering itself
  • Full strip, re-deck and re-cover where the timber underneath has gone soft

Flat roofing questions we get asked in Glastonbury

Do you cover the villages around Glastonbury?

Yes. Baltonsborough, Butleigh, Meare, West Pennard and the surrounding lanes are all within our normal working area, as is anywhere else in BA6.

The ground here is low-lying. Does that affect a flat roof?

Not the roof directly, but properties on the Levels often deal with damp from more than one direction. If we survey a roof and think what you are seeing inside is coming from somewhere other than the roof, we will say so rather than sell you a roof you do not need.

Can you re-cover a flat roof without stripping it first?

Sometimes, if the existing deck and covering are sound. Often it is a false economy, because it hides problems and adds weight. We will tell you at the survey which of the two your roof is.
