What Wells rooftops are usually like

Wells has an unusual mix for a place its size. The centre is dense with Georgian and Victorian stone-built property, much of it inside the conservation area and a good deal of it listed, where flat roofs tend to be small, awkward and hidden: box gutters behind parapets, lead-lined valleys, porch and bay window tops, and outriggers tucked behind the main roofline.

Around that centre sit the post-war and later estates, where the flat roofs are the more familiar kind. Attached garages, kitchen and dining room extensions, flat-roofed dormers and the occasional full flat-roofed bungalow. These are the roofs most often reaching the end of their original felt covering now, and they are the ones GRP and EPDM suit best.

What we get called out for in Wells

Which covering is best for a Wells extension roof?

It depends on the deck, the size, the detailing and whether the roof gets walked on. GRP gives a hard, seamless finish and suits roofs that see foot traffic; EPDM is a single sheet with very few joins and copes well with movement. We will tell you which we would use on your roof and why at the survey.
